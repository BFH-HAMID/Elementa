key_points:
  - "Aim: Measure the temperature rise when a strong acid neutralises a strong base and calculate the enthalpy of neutralisation per mole of water formed."
  - "Apparatus: insulated cup calorimeter, standard HCl and NaOH, temperature probe, measuring cylinders."
  - "Principle: For dilute strong acid and base, $H^{+} + OH^{-} \\rightarrow H_{2}O$; at roughly constant pressure, $q_{\\mathrm{solution}} \\approx (m_{\\mathrm{sol}}c + C_{\\mathrm{c}})\\Delta T$ and $\\Delta H_{\\mathrm{neutralization}} \\approx -\\frac{q_{\\mathrm{solution}}}{n_{\\mathrm{water}}}$ for the water formed."
  - "Relation to use: $H^{+} + OH^{-} \\rightarrow H_{2}O$."
  - "Key step: Measure initial reagent temperatures, mix known volumes rapidly in insulated cup, stir and log temperature versus time to estimate corrected peak."
  - "Record: acid C and V, base C and V, initial T (°C), peak T (°C), mixture mass (g) for every trial, with units."
  - "Calculation: Compute the moles of water from the limiting $H^{+}$ or $OH^{-}$; $\\Delta H = -\\frac{(m_{\\mathrm{sol}}c + C_{\\mathrm{c}})\\Delta T}{n_{\\mathrm{water}}}$."
  - "Repeat: Repeat at least three independent trials, preserve raw readings, compute the result with units and compare the observed trend against the stated model."
  - "Take care: Wear eye protection; both strong acid and base can burn skin."
  - "Error sources: Heat leakage, unequal initial temperatures, nonzero cup heat capacity and incomplete mixing bias $\\Delta H$."
  - "Viva: Why is $\\Delta H$ negative for exothermic neutralization?"
