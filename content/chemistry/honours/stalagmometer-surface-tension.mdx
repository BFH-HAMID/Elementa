  - "Aim: Compare the surface tension of a liquid with that of water by counting the drops a stalagmometer delivers for a fixed volume."
  - "Apparatus: stalagmometer, reference liquid, unknown liquid, thermometer."
  - "Principle: For the same stalagmometer volume and tip, surface tension approximately follows $\\gamma _{1}/\\gamma _{2}\\approx \\rho _{1}\\frac{n_{2}}{\\rho _{2}n_{1}}$, where n is drops for that volume; drop correction and wetting may matter."
  - "Relation to use: $\\gamma _{1}/\\gamma _{2}\\approx \\rho _{1}\\frac{n_{2}}{\\rho _{2}n_{1}}$."
  - "Key step: Clean tip and count slow drops between calibration marks for each liquid at identical temperature; repeat without changing tip geometry."
  - "Record: liquid, density (kg/m³), drop count n, temperature (°C), surface tension ratio for every trial, with units."
  - "Calculation: $\\frac{\\gamma_{\\mathrm{unknown}}}{\\gamma_{\\mathrm{ref}}} = \\frac{\\rho_{\\mathrm{unknown}}n_{\\mathrm{ref}}}{\\rho_{\\mathrm{ref}}n_{\\mathrm{unknown}}}$ under the drop-count approximation; report the limitation of the method."
  - "Repeat: Repeat at least three independent trials, preserve raw readings, compute the result with units and compare the observed trend against the stated model."
  - "Take care: Do not mouth-pipette; fragile glass and solvent vapour require care and ventilation."
  - "Error sources: Fast dropping, contaminated tip, variable contact angle and density errors bias result."
  - "Viva: At fixed volume, more drops generally imply what about average drop mass?"
