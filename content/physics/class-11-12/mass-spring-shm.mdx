theory: "For a light ideal spring and small vertical oscillations about equilibrium, the restoring force is −kx and the effective oscillating mass m gives $\\omega =\\sqrt{k/m}$, hence $T=2\\pi \\sqrt{m/k}$. A plot of $T^{2}$ against total attached mass has slope $\\frac{4\\pi^{2}}{k}$."
theory_bn: "হালকা আদর্শ স্প্রিংয়ে সাম্যাবস্থার চারপাশে ছোট উল্লম্ব দোলনে প্রত্যাবর্তী বল −kx এবং কার্যকর দোলায়মান ভর m হলে $\\omega =\\sqrt{k/m}$, তাই $T=2\\pi \\sqrt{m/k}$। মোট সংযুক্ত ভরের বিপরীতে $T^{2}$ আঁকলে ঢাল $\\frac{4\\pi^{2}}{k}$।"
procedure:
  - Clamp the spring securely and measure its unloaded length; place a tray below the hanger.
  - Add a small mass, wait for vertical motion to settle, then displace it slightly and release without twisting.
  - Time 20 complete oscillations; repeat twice and divide the mean time by 20 to find T.
  - Repeat for at least four masses while staying within the spring’s elastic limit.
  - "Plot $T^{2}$ against total attached mass and use the slope to estimate k."
