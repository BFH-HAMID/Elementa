theory: "For small angular amplitudes, a simple pendulum behaves approximately as $T = 2\\pi \\sqrt{(L/g)}$. Squaring gives $T^{2} = (\\frac{4\\pi^{2}}{g})L$, so the slope of $a T^{2}-L$ graph is $\\frac{4\\pi^{2}}{g}$."
theory_bn: "ছোট কৌণিক বিস্তারে সরল দোলকের জন্য $T = 2\\pi \\sqrt{(L/g)}$। তাই $T^{2} = (4\\pi ^{2}/g)L$; $T^{2}-L$ গ্রাফের slope হলো $\\frac{4\\pi^{2}}{g}$।"
procedure:
  - Set the bob length from the support to the centre of the bob and record it.
  - Displace the bob by a small angle and release without pushing.
  - Time 20 complete oscillations, then divide by 20 for T.
  - Repeat for at least four lengths and average repeated readings.
  - "Plot $T^{2}$ against L and calculate g from the slope."
