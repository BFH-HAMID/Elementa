key_points:
  - "Aim: Find the Young modulus of a beam material from its central deflection under known loads, plotting deflection against force."
  - "Apparatus: beam and two supports, central hanger, dial gauge, vernier caliper."
  - "Principle: A simply supported rectangular beam with central load F has centre deflection $\\delta =\\frac{FL^{3}}{48YI}, I=\\frac{bd^{3}}{12}$; hence $Y=\\frac{FL^{3}}{4bd^{3}\\delta }$."
  - "Relation to use: $\\delta =\\frac{FL^{3}}{48YI}, I=\\frac{bd^{3}}{12}$."
  - "Key step: Measure span L, width b and depth d; add known central loads and record equilibrium central deflections on loading and unloading."
  - "Record: load F (N), span L (m), width b (m), depth d (m), deflection $\\delta$ (m) for every trial, with units."
  - "Calculation: Plot $\\delta$ against F: if slope is $s=\\frac{\\delta}{F}, Y=\\frac{L^{3}}{4bd^{3}s}$; report uncertainty especially in $d^{3}$."
  - "Repeat: Repeat at least three independent trials, preserve raw readings, compute the result with units and compare the observed trend against the stated model."
  - "Take care: Secure supports and keep deflection small enough for elastic response."
  - "Error sources: Support slippage, shear deflection and large-load plasticity change the result."
  - "Viva: Which beam dimension is cubed in the denominator?"
